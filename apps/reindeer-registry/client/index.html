Reindeer Registry Overview

Reindeer Registry helps you protect the things that matter most — and makes things easier for the people you love when they need it most. You were invited here because someone who cares about you wants to make sure your wishes are known and your family is supported.

Purpose

Reindeer has two simple goals:

  • Help you create a personal property memorandum, an often overlooked but important part of an estate plan
  • Build an inventory of belongings your family may one day need to manage

The first is about honoring your wishes. The second is about caring for the people you leave behind.

When someone passes, it is rarely the financial assets that create the hardest moments — it is the everyday things filled with memory and meaning. A watch worn for decades, a favorite chair, a painting on the wall, a tool in the garage, a keepsake from childhood. These are the items that carry stories, and it is not always clear who might cherish them.

How It Works

Reindeer Registry is designed to be simple and gentle to use.

Start with the items you already know matter. Take a photo, name it, and say who it is for. That becomes your personal property memorandum — the most important part of this.

You do not need to catalog your whole house. Most people include fewer than thirty specific items. If you do nothing else, document those.

When you are ready, you can also walk through the rest of the house room by room. Reindeer helps identify what it sees and organizes it into your inventory. This is valuable, but it is secondary — the memorandum comes first.

When the time comes, your wishes come first. Anything not specifically assigned can be handled through Reindeer: FairPlay, which helps your family divide remaining items with clarity, fairness, and less conflict.

Take your time and let Reindeer guide you. A small effort today can bring great peace later — and help ensure the meaning behind your belongings is not lost.

Legal Notice

Reindeer Registry is not a legal instrument. Your completed and signed personal property memorandum is the document that governs your wishes.

Its quiet gift is peace.

Making your list official

This is the one thing you cannot do on a screen.

The law that lets people sign contracts and tax returns electronically has a short list of things it deliberately leaves out. Wills are on that list — and so is any paper that takes effect through a will, which is what your list does.

So there is no Sign here button in this app. A signature typed or drawn on a screen would look finished and then fail at the one moment it was ever needed. You need to print the page and sign it with a pen.

It is three minutes of work. Then it is done.

  1. 1

    Print the page

    It lists every item where you have said who it is for, and it has a line at the bottom for your signature.

  2. 2

    Sign it with a pen, and date it

    Your own signature is all that is required. Witnesses are optional, never a bad idea, and there are lines for two of them if you want to use them.

  3. 3

    Put the signed page with your will

    Keep it wherever the will itself is kept. This matters: if the signed page cannot be found later, a court can assume you tore it up on purpose.

  4. 4

    Take a photo of the signed page

    Then your trustee can see that it exists and what it says, without anyone having to move the original.

Say it plainly enough that someone who has never been in your house could find it.

Say it in your own voice

This is the part a court is most likely to find persuasive. A recording cannot be a will — but it is good evidence that you meant it, that you understood it, and that nobody was standing over you.

Something like:

“My name is your name. Today is the date. I have just read and signed the list of my belongings, and it says what I want to happen to them. Nobody has told me what to put in it.”

Keep it short and unhurried, and record it in one go — an unedited recording carries more weight than a polished one. If you are having a foggy day, do it another day instead.

Has a professional confirmed it?

If a trustee or attorney has seen the signed original, record it here. Their confirmation is worth more than the photograph on its own.

Rules differ from state to state, and a few states give a list like this no effect at all. Your own attorney can tell you in one sentence which you are in.
