Welcome to Reindeer Registry

A loving place to begin.

Someone who cares about you — your estate attorney, trustee, financial advisor, or a trusted friend — sent you here because they want to make things easier for you and for the people you love.

Reindeer Registry helps you name who should receive the belongings closest to your heart. Along the way, it becomes a tender portrait of the home you’ve made.

You don’t need to account for everything. Most memorandums include fewer than thirty wishes. Begin with the pieces that tug at you first — the ones with a story, memory, or feeling you want someone to understand.

Family uncertainty often begins in small moments — a drawer opened later, an object no one recognizes, a memory someone wishes they could ask about. Setting your stories down is a way of staying close.

Reindeer Registry preserves the wishes you name. Everything else is supported through Reindeer: FairPlay — where your wishes are honored first, and your family is gently guided through what remains.

Reindeer Registry isn’t the legal instrument itself; your signed memorandum is what governs. Change your mind anytime. Nothing moves forward until you’re ready.

How to use Reindeer Registry

This app helps you create a personal property memorandum — a loving record of who should receive the belongings closest to your heart.

Photograph items. Point the camera at an item and take a close-up so details are clearly visible. The app will try to identify it for you — accept the name or type your own.

Photograph rooms. For each room, take a few wider shots from different angles. These give a sense of the whole space and help your family recognize where things belong. The app will automatically identify items it sees and add them to your list.

Name who gets it. Choose someone from your people list, or add a new name. That’s a wish — the app records it.

Tell its story. Write a sentence or two about why the item matters, or use your voice to add detail. This is the part that means the most to the people who come after you.

Work room by room. Use “The rooms in your house” to go through one room at a time. The app automatically identifies items in each photo and adds them to your list. It keeps track of where you left off.

Mark important items. When something is important, mark it. The app will ask for a close-up photo so the details are captured clearly.

Sign and deliver. When you’re ready, sign your memorandum and send it to your trustee or to Reindeer: FairPlay. Nothing moves forward until you are ready.

Making your list official

This is the one thing you cannot do on a screen.

The law that lets people sign contracts and tax returns electronically has a short list of things it deliberately leaves out. Wills are on that list — and so is any paper that takes effect through a will, which is what your list does.

So there is no Sign here button in this app. A signature typed or drawn on a screen would look finished and then fail at the one moment it was ever needed. You need to print the page and sign it with a pen.

Only a signed paper document carries any legal weight. The photograph you take in the next step is for documentation only — it shows your trustee that the signed page exists and what it says. It is not a substitute for the signed original.

It is three minutes of work. Then it is done.

  1. 1

    Print the page

    It lists every item where you have said who it is for, and it has a line at the bottom for your signature.

  2. 2

    Sign it with a pen, and date it

    Your own signature is all that is required. Witnesses are optional, never a bad idea, and there are lines for two of them if you want to use them.

  3. 3

    Put the signed page with your will

    Keep it wherever the will itself is kept. This matters: if the signed page cannot be found later, a court can assume you tore it up on purpose.

  4. 4

    Take a photo of the signed page

    This photograph is for documentation only — it shows your trustee that the signed page exists and what it says. It does not replace the signed original, which is the only copy with legal weight.

Say it plainly enough that someone who has never been in your house could find it.

Say it in your own voice

This is the part a court is most likely to find persuasive. A recording cannot be a will — but it is good evidence that you meant it, that you understood it, and that nobody was standing over you.

Something like:

“My name is your name. Today is the date. I have just read and signed the list of my belongings, and it says what I want to happen to them. Nobody has told me what to put in it.”

Keep it short and unhurried, and record it in one go — an unedited recording carries more weight than a polished one. If you are having a foggy day, do it another day instead.

Has a professional confirmed it?

If a trustee or attorney has seen the signed original, record it here. Their confirmation is worth more than the photograph on its own.

Rules differ from state to state, and a few states give a list like this no effect at all. Your own attorney can tell you in one sentence which you are in.

Important — don’t skip this

  1. Generate a license key and send it to your trustee. Go to Estate license keys on the home screen to generate a key. The key lets your trustee open the .reindeer file in Reindeer: FairPlay. Print the attorney/trustee letter (marked privileged and confidential) and deliver it with the estate documents. Share the key separately from the file — in person or by phone, not by email.
  2. Give the data file to your trustee and/or the lawyer holding your will. Your .reindeer file should be stored with your estate planning documents. Send it to your trustee, your attorney, or both — whoever you trust to keep it safe and hand it over at the right time. Tell them it is your personal property memorandum referenced by your will.
  3. Keep your own copy somewhere safe. Download a copy to a place your trustee will know to look — a labeled USB drive, a safe deposit box, or a folder your executor is aware of. The file is no good to anyone if they can’t find it.
  4. The key and the file must travel separately. If someone finds the file, they cannot open it without the key. If someone has the key but no file, they cannot read your wishes. Give the key to your trustee by phone or in person. Send the file to your trustee and/or lawyer by any secure means. Your will can reference both.

These instructions are also printed on the last page of your printable list, so a paper copy goes wherever your estate papers go.
