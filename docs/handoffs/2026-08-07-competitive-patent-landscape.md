## 0. Simplified patentability read (plain English)

**Added 2026-08-07 after the full research pass. This section is a screening read for the owner, not counsel's opinion. Read §1 for the full analysis with citations.**

### The short answer

A handful of mechanics look genuinely patentable, but **not the ones you'd probably guess**. The AI photo pipeline, the offline sync, and the ranked draft are all crowded. The parts that look most defensible are the ones about *what the paper does and does not say*, the *signed method-agreement gate*, and the *cross-app provenance chain*.

Two caveats apply to every "yes" below:

1. This was a screening scan through Google Patents result pages and Justia assignee queries. It is not a professional search. EPO/WIPO family checks, CPC-class sweeps (G06Q 50/16, G06Q 40/00), and file-wrapper review are still required.
2. Every mechanic here sits in **business-method territory** under 35 USC §101. Novelty is only half the game — eligibility is the other half. The print-rendering rule (R4) and the offline-capture behavior are the parts most likely to survive §101 because they read as concrete machine-implemented steps rather than abstract ideas.

### Strong on their own — novel and with a technical hook

| # | Mechanic | Why it holds up |
|---|---|---|
| **R2 + R3 + R4** | Owner-authored "Important" with three signals; comment prints verbatim; reason chip suppressed from paper | **The suppression rule is the key.** No fetched patent claims a display/print rule that deliberately omits a stored classification code from the rendered artifact. That is a concrete machine-implemented rendering step — a real §101 hook. |
| **F1** | Every heir signs a versioned, immutable snapshot of the method text before ranking can open | Signing-ceremony patents exist but none *gates a division engine* on unanimous signature of a versioned rules snapshot. Delivered by single-use short-lived magic link adds a second technical limitation. |
| **F6** | Finalization completes with pending appraisals, and the printed Record names which heir raised each open appraisal | Nothing found. Specific, unusual state-machine behavior plus a specific print rule. |
| **F3 + F7** together | Cross-app import creates an audit row attributed to the *import*, not any participant; reverts persist as two preserved rows | No fetched product exports an owner flag between two apps. The nearest fiduciary-control art is abandoned and does not recite this. |

### Only patentable as combinations or dependent limitations

| # | Mechanic | The problem alone, the value in combination |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | Two-list only, no "undecided" bucket | Negative limitation. Cannot carry a claim by itself, but it is a clean dependent claim inside a larger one. |
| R5 | Never estimates, never requests appraisal | Interesting *because* someone else (US11861722B2) claims the inverse — auto-predicting a user's possessions with values attached. "Hard-suppress any monetary estimate" becomes a real distinguishing limitation, but only inside a bigger claim. |
| F4 | Heir-triggered off-platform appraisal, internal AI estimate hidden from heirs | FairSplit's US8812389B2 already recites appraisal requests. The **hidden-estimate** limitation is the novel bit — was not found anywhere — but it needs company. |
| F9 | Server-rendered Record of Decisions, auto-print, trustee handoff | Distinguishing only as the terminal step of the F1 + F3 + F6 chain. |

### Weak — do not lead with these

| # | Mechanic | Why |
|---|---|---|
| F2 | Ranked draft over non-high-value items | **Most exposed of all.** FairSplit's expired-but-still-prior-art US8812389B2 covers exactly this domain, plus US8577748B1, US20080235113A1, and the auction-theory family. Do not build a claim around this. |
| R6 | AI photo → structured item | Crowded. Encircle AI, Heirly, FairSplit AI quick listing all shipped. US10856807B2 is active to 2038 and covers image → item analysis via ML. |
| R10 | Offline IndexedDB + service worker | Generic. US20210118435A1 and US11042883B2 cover offline-then-sync at the class level. |
| R7 | Duplicate detection by title + photo hash | Textbook record deduplication. |
| R8 | End-of-room "special meaning" prompt | UI nudge, not an invention. |
| R9 | Signed paper is authoritative | Legal convention. Fabric and LegalZoom sell "print and sign" as ordinary practice. US20150207786A1 already articulates tangible-receipt-as-authority. |
| F5 | No aggregate high-value threshold | Negative limitation, cannot stand alone. |
| F8 | Cookie-only auth, deny-by-default | Security best practice. |
| F10 | Practice mode | Sandbox flag. |

### One-sentence recommendation for counsel

**Do not file around the ranked-draft algorithm.** File a single independent claim spanning both apps: the intake app records the three-signal Important flag, the paper reproduces the comment verbatim while suppressing the reason code and any monetary estimate, an export envelope carries the flag across the app boundary, the receiving app attributes it as an import (not a participant) and refuses to open ranking until every heir has signed a versioned immutable snapshot, and finalization emits a printed record naming who raised any still-open appraisal. Every distinguishing limitation in that shape is either not found in the fetched prior art or is stated in the opposite direction.

Two useful narrower fallbacks: **F3 + F7** as an audit-provenance mechanism, and **F4 + F6** as the appraisal handling — internal estimate used only for routing, never rendered to heirs, coexisting with an off-platform human appraisal that does not block finalization.

## 0.5 The architectural argument (two-audience split)

Added 2026-08-07. This section reframes the patent posture. The strongest case is not any one mechanic — it is the **two-audience architecture** that forces the mechanics to exist. Read this before §1 if time is short.

### The reframing

Reindeer Legacy is not one app with two modes. It is two applications with two different target users and two different design languages, joined by a one-way envelope:

- **Registry (intake)** is scoped to a **single elderly owner** authoring items at their own pace. Large type, plain language, 44px touch targets, confirm-before-irreversible, printable at every step. Owner authors an "Important" flag with an optional free-text comment. **The app never estimates value and never requests appraisal.**
- **FairPlay (division)** is scoped to **multiple adult heirs** processing grief and family dynamics after the owner is gone. Signed method-agreement gate, ranked draft over non-high-value items, heir-triggered off-platform appraisal, hidden internal AI estimate used only for routing. **Valuations are secondary; emotional bindings are primary.**
- **The envelope (ReindeerExchange v1)** carries the owner's flag and free-text comment across the app boundary. The receiving app records the arrival as an audit row attributed to the *import*, not to any heir participant. The owner never has to defend the flag in a live heir dispute.

### What every fetched competitor does instead

Every competitor in the fetched set has **one user model applied uniformly**, and none of them positions itself the way described above:

| Camp | Products | User model | Emotion handling |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Division** | [FairSplit](https://www.fairsplit.com/), [Estate Divvy](https://www.estatedivvy.com/how-it-works), [Nemu](https://www.mynemu.com/estate-management), [Heirly](https://heirly.app/) | Owner, executor, and heirs use the same interface with the same scoring/bidding/points UI | Compressed to a number: emotional-value credits ([FairSplit EV round](https://manual.fairsplit.com/division/round-ev.html)), 10,000-point budgets ([Estate Divvy](https://www.estatedivvy.com/how-it-works)), proprietary emotional-value scores ([Nemu](https://www.mynemu.com/estate-management)) |
| **Inventory** | [SaveOr](https://www.saveor.com/estate-planning), [Binary Formations](https://binaryformations.com/products/home-inventory/), [Sortly](https://www.sortly.com/), [Encircle](https://www.getencircle.com/solutions/contents/), [Cratify](https://thecratesapp.com/for/families/) | Owner-facing only, no heir side | Notes as freeform fields; not printed as an authoritative instrument |
| **Document organizers** | [Everplans](https://www.everplans.com/what-is-everplans), [Trustworthy](https://www.trustworthy.com/), [Nokbox](https://www.thenokbox.com/), [Cake](https://www.joincake.com/), [Fabric](https://www.meetfabric.com/), [LegalZoom](https://www.legalzoom.com/), [WillMaker](https://www.willmaker.com/) | Documents only; neither item-emotional nor item-physical | n.a. |

**Nobody splits by audience.** Nobody says "the intake app is deliberately designed for an elderly owner at a kitchen table, the division app is deliberately designed for adult heirs processing grief, and neither app uses money as the primary language."

### Why the four novel mechanics are consequences of the split

The four mechanics flagged as "strong on their own" in §0 are not four random details. **They are all direct consequences of the two-audience architecture.** Which makes the architectural claim stronger than any individual mechanic claim.

| Novel mechanic | Why the two-audience frame requires it |
|---|---|
| **R2 + R3 + R4** — paper says only "Important," comment prints verbatim, reason code suppressed | The elderly owner has to trust the paper without reading a dashboard. Suppressing dollar figures and reason codes protects the owner from having to explain themselves. |
| **F1** — signed versioned method-agreement gate before ranking opens | The heir audience is emotionally activated. They must agree to the rules of engagement *before* rankings, not have rules imposed after. |
| **F3 + F7** — cross-app import as null-participant audit row, reverts preserved as two rows | The owner's authorship must be preserved across an app boundary without pulling the owner into a live heir dispute. "The owner said so" is captured architecturally. |
| **F6** — finalization completes with pending appraisals, printed record names the requesting heir | If money were primary, you'd block finalization on appraisal. Because emotion is primary, appraisal is an escape valve, not a gate. |

### Is "elderly-friendly UI" itself patentable? No — and that is not the claim.

Big type, 44px touch targets, plain language, and "confirm before irreversible" are accessibility best practice. They would not survive a §101 or novelty attack on their own. **Nobody patents "our app has big buttons."**

What *is* patentable is the **architectural consequence** of the two audiences, not the styling. Specifically:

1. **Role-based data suppression as a rendering rule.** The system stores reason codes and (in FairPlay) computes AI value estimates, but the elderly-owner-facing print output strips reason codes and monetary estimates, and the heir-facing display never shows the internal AI estimate. This is a machine-implemented rendering rule tied to the user's role, and no fetched patent recites it.
2. **A two-application envelope carrying an owner-authored flag across an audience boundary.** Intake is authored by an owner in a low-conflict setting. Division is used by heirs in a high-conflict setting. The envelope preserves the owner's word while removing the owner from the conflict. This is architectural, not stylistic.
3. **"High-value" as an emotion-protective device, not a financial one.** Every other product treats high-value as "these items need special appraisal because money." Reindeer Legacy treats it as "these items get pulled out of the ranked draft because their emotional weight would poison the draft for lower-stakes items." The framing is inverted from the field.

### The reframed one-sentence independent claim for counsel

**A two-application estate personal-property system in which the intake application is scoped to a single owner authoring per-item flags without valuation, the division application is scoped to multiple heir participants ranking items under a signed versioned method agreement, an export envelope carries the owner's flag and free-text comment across the application boundary and stores the arrival as an audit record attributed to the import rather than any participant, the rendered artifacts in each application suppress role-inappropriate stored fields — the owner's paper omits any monetary estimate and any classification reason code, and the heir display omits any internal automatic value estimate — and finalization of the division proceeds while third-party appraisals remain outstanding, with the identity of the requesting heir recorded on the printed decision.**

That single claim carries the two-audience architecture, the four novel mechanics from §0, and the emotion-primary / value-secondary framing all at once. Every distinguishing limitation is either not present in the fetched prior art or stated in the opposite direction.

### One caveat specific to this reframing

The screening scan did not run a dedicated query for role-based UI patents or assistive-technology / accessibility patents. That category exists (accessible-computing patents, adaptive-interface patents) and counsel should sweep it before filing. But **none of the 21 estate/inventory products in the fetched set positions itself as a two-audience split**, and none of the 38 fetched patent records recites a role-partitioned intake-vs-division architecture. On this record, the framing is unique.

## 1. Executive summary

Across 21 fetched products and 38 fetched patent records, the tangible-personal-property space splits cleanly into three camps: **document organizers** that never touch physical items ([Everplans](https://www.everplans.com/what-is-everplans), [Trustworthy](https://www.trustworthy.com/), [Nokbox](https://www.thenokbox.com/), [Cake](https://www.joincake.com/)), **inventory tools** that photograph items but have no heirs and no division ([Sortly](https://www.sortly.com/), [Encircle](https://www.getencircle.com/solutions/contents/)), and a small **division** camp — [FairSplit](https://www.fairsplit.com/how-it-works/), [Estate Divvy](https://www.estatedivvy.com/how-it-works), [Heirly](https://heirly.app/), [Nemu](https://www.mynemu.com/estate-management) — every one of which runs on **points, credits, or scores that reduce sentiment to a number**. FairSplit gives each divisee a fixed pool of "emotional value" credits for blind bidding ([FairSplit manual, EV round](https://manual.fairsplit.com/division/round-ev.html)); Estate Divvy gives each participant **10,000 points** to spend ([Estate Divvy how it works](https://www.estatedivvy.com/how-it-works)); Nemu collects "emotional value scores" and runs a proprietary formula ([Nemu estate management](https://www.mynemu.com/estate-management)).

Five mechanics stand out as most likely to differentiate in market **and** to be worth a patent attorney's attention:

1. **R2 + R3 + R4 as one interlocked rule — the owner's word "Important," never a valuation.** Every competitor in the division camp converts sentiment into a bid, a score, or a point budget. No fetched product uses a three-signal, owner-authored flag whose free text prints verbatim while the *reason* is deliberately withheld from paper. No fetched patent claims a display/print rule that suppresses a stored classification reason from the printed artifact.
2. **R1's strict two-list model with no "undecided" bucket.** The nearest analog does the opposite: Binary Formations' Home Inventory assigns heirs *and* keeps an explicit "(unassigned)" bucket ([Binary Formations user guide](https://binaryformations.com/homeinventory/HomeInventoryUserGuide.pdf)). Absence-of-a-state is a hard thing to claim on its own, but as a limitation inside a larger claim it is unusually clean.
3. **F1's Method Agreement gate — immutable per-row snapshot + version signed by every heir before ranking opens.** Fetched e-signature patents cover signing ceremonies and multi-contributor workflows ([US9286596B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9286596B2/en), [US20190050587A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20190050587A1/en), [US20240104299A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20240104299A1/en)) but none was found to gate a *property-division round engine* on unanimous signature of a versioned rules snapshot.
4. **F4 + F6 together — heir-triggered escalation to an off-platform human appraiser, with finalization permitted while appraisals are still pending.** FairSplit's own patent recites appraisal requests and a monetary-valuation round ([US8812389B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8812389B2/en)), so escalation alone is not clear ground; the *finalize-with-pending-appraisal* artifact naming the escalating heir was not found in any fetched patent.
5. **F3 + F7 + F9 — a cross-app provenance chain that ends on paper.** An owner-authored flag crossing an app boundary (ReindeerExchange v1) into a division engine as a null-participant audit row, revertible by the PR as two preserved rows, and terminating in a printed Record of Decisions, was not found in any fetched competitor or patent.

Conversely, three mechanics look weak on their own: **F2 (ranked draft)** — snake/selection-order drafting is FairSplit's shipped product ([FairSplit manual, SO round](https://manual.fairsplit.com/division/round-so.html)) and is claimed generically in [US8577748B1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8577748B1); **R6 (AI photo → item)** — FairSplit added AI quick listing in 2024 ([FairSplit about](https://www.fairsplit.com/about/)), Encircle AI identifies items from a single photo ([Encircle contents](https://www.getencircle.com/solutions/contents/)), Heirly suggests title and value from a photo ([Heirly](https://heirly.app/)), and image-recognition item identification is claimed in [US10856807B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10856807B2/en) and [US9495386B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9495386B2/en); and **R10 (offline sync)** — generic offline-then-sync is long claimed, e.g. [US11042883B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11042883B2/en) and [US20210118435A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20210118435A1/en).

One commercial note: **FairSplit's patent, US8812389B2, is listed as "Expired - Fee Related"** with a lapse date of 2022-08-19 ([US8812389B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8812389B2/en)), even though fairsplit.com still markets "its own patented method" ([FairSplit manual, concepts](https://manual.fairsplit.com/about/concepts.html)) and cites the number on its how-it-works page ([FairSplit how it works](https://www.fairsplit.com/how-it-works/)). Counsel should verify status at USPTO directly; a lapsed patent is still prior art even if it is no longer enforceable.

### A.2 Per-product narrative — and which of the 20 mechanics each lacks

**FairSplit** is the only direct, mature competitor to FairPlay and the only one with a patent on file. Its process is explicitly round-based: an **Asset Review** round where divisees mark yes/no interest, an **Emotional Value** round in which each divisee receives a fixed credit budget (the manual's worked example uses 500) and places blind silent-auction bids on up to 15 items, with the highest EV bid winning and ties broken by a random coin flip while the item's fair market value is charged to the winner's account ([EV round](https://manual.fairsplit.com/division/round-ev.html)), and a **Selection Order** round where divisees rank every remaining item in descending preference and a snake order (random or admin-set) resolves the picks ([SO round](https://manual.fairsplit.com/division/round-so.html)). The company describes this as "its own patented method" ([concepts](https://manual.fairsplit.com/about/concepts.html)) and adopted AI quick listing in 2024 ([about](https://www.fairsplit.com/about/)). A third-party overview corroborates the three-round structure ([Executorium](https://executorium.com/solutions/fair-split/)), and the manual offers strategy guidance to divisees on spending their credits ([strategies](https://manual.fairsplit.com/division/strategies.html), [using FairSplit](https://manual.fairsplit.com/using.html)).

What FairSplit does **not** have, on the pages fetched: **R1** (no two-list-only model — manual assignment coexists with the rounds, [help](https://fairsplit.zendesk.com/hc/en-us)), **R2/R3/R4** (sentiment is expressed as a *bid budget*, not an owner-authored word that prints verbatim while its reason is withheld), **R5** (FairSplit is value-centric: it advises discounting appraised values 40–50%, [resources](https://manual.fairsplit.com/about/resources.html)), **R7**, **R8**, **R10**, **F1** (no unanimous signed method-agreement gate found), **F3** (no cross-app owner-import provenance), **F4** as an heir-triggered in-app escalation to an off-platform appraiser, **F5**, **F6**, **F7**, **F8**, **F9** as a signed Record of Decisions, and **F10** (no practice mode found). Its equalization stance is closest to the owner's "Fair, not Equal": it treats 5–10% variance as acceptable rather than forcing dollar parity ([resources](https://manual.fairsplit.com/about/resources.html)).

**Estate Divvy** is the purest expression of the points paradigm: participants score a locked list by spending 10,000 points, scoring is private, and results are released once ([how it works](https://www.estatedivvy.com/how-it-works)). It therefore directly conflicts with **R2–R5** (sentiment is quantified) and lacks **R1, R6–R10, F1, F3–F10**. It does share **F2**'s spirit — a preference-driven allocation — but as a single-shot optimisation rather than a ranked draft.

**Heirly** is the closest to FairPlay's *shape*: executor-run rounds, separate sentimental and financial rounds, an executor with final say, a transparent ledger, and a full PDF report with audit export ([heirly.app](https://heirly.app/), [pricing](https://heirly.app/pricing)). But it explicitly leans equalizing — it ships a settlement calculator — which is precisely what the owner's "Fair, not Equal" rule rejects. It also AI-estimates value from a photo and *shows* it, whereas FairPlay's internal estimate is never shown to heirs (**F4**). Missing: **R1–R5, R7, R8, R10, F1, F3, F5, F6, F8, F10**.

**Nemu** collects emotional value scores and runs them through a proprietary formula, with the number of division rounds gated by subscription tier ([estate management](https://www.mynemu.com/estate-management), [pricing](https://www.mynemu.com/pricing)). It is the only competitor besides Registry to capture *stories and voice notes* on items — but the fetched pages do not state that those stories print verbatim on a memorandum, so **R3** remains distinct. Missing: **R1, R2, R4, R5, R7–R10, F1, F3, F5–F10**.

**SaveOr** is the closest analog to Registry's *output*: photo-backed inventory, direct heir assignment, per-item notes about history and significance, and export of "a properly formatted PPM" ([estate planning](https://www.saveor.com/estate-planning)). Two gaps matter. First, it does not state that the notes appear on the exported memorandum — the notes-print-verbatim rule (**R3**) is unconfirmed there. Second, it has no division method at all; its heir-side feature is private flagging ([millennial inheritance](https://www.saveor.com/millennial-inheritance-management-app)). Missing: **R1, R2, R4, R5, R7, R8, R10, F1–F10**.

**Binary Formations Home Inventory** is the single most useful contrast for **R1**. It assigns an heir per item and prints a per-heir report suitable as a will addendum — but it maintains an explicit **"(unassigned)"** bucket ([user guide](https://binaryformations.com/homeinventory/HomeInventoryUserGuide.pdf)). Registry's design decision to forbid that third state is therefore a genuine, demonstrable departure from the closest shipped product, not a hypothetical one. Missing: **R2, R4–R8, R10, F1–F10**.

**Cratify** assigns items to family members but as ownership records inside a household inventory, with no estate framing and no division ([families](https://thecratesapp.com/for/families/)). Missing: essentially all 20 mechanics except a weak **R1**-adjacent assignment field.

**Everplans, Trustworthy, Nokbox, Cake, Afterly Plan, Fabric, Wealth.com, LegalZoom, WillMaker/Nolo, Estateably, EstateExec and Snug** collectively define the negative space. None of them captures item photographs with descriptions, none assigns tangible items to heirs at item level (LegalZoom's "Schedule of assets" and WillMaker's unlimited specific bequests are textual, not photographic), and **none has any post-death division method among multiple heirs**. They therefore lack all 20 mechanics with three partial exceptions worth noting: WillMaker's free-form "Letter to Survivors" is a distant cousin of **R3** ([what Nolo's online will can do](https://www.willmaker.com/legal-manual/wills/what-nolo-s-online-will-can-do.html)); Fabric's "A printer, a pen, two witnesses" and LegalZoom's "Then print and sign" show that **R9**'s paper-is-authoritative stance is conventional in will software ([Fabric wills](https://meetfabric.com/wills), [LegalZoom bundle](https://www.legalzoom.com/personal/estate-planning/estate-planning-bundle.html)); and Nolo's own explainer confirms the personal property memorandum is a recognised, signed, will-referenced instrument ([Nolo PPM article](https://www.nolo.com/legal-encyclopedia/using-personal-property-memorandum-with-your-will.html)).

**Sortly and Encircle** matter only as evidence that **R6** is commoditised. Encircle states that its AI automatically identifies items from a single photo and extracts descriptions, brands and models ([contents](https://www.getencircle.com/solutions/contents/)); Sortly ships item photos and custom fields ([pricing](https://www.sortly.com/pricing/)). Neither has heirs, division, memoranda, or appraisal escalation.

### B.2 Analysis

**Which mechanics appear novel on this record.** Four clusters had no close hit in any of the 38 fetched patents. First, **R2 + R3 + R4** — an owner-authored classification with three independent signals whose *free text prints verbatim* while its *reason code is deliberately suppressed from the printed artifact*. Selective suppression of a stored field from a rendered document is a concrete, machine-implemented display rule; none of the fetched records recites anything comparable, and none of the fetched estate patents even mentions a printed memorandum ([US8812389B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8812389B2/en) explicitly does not state printed memorandum). Second, **F1** — gating a division engine on unanimous heir signature of an *immutable per-row snapshot with a version identifier*, delivered by single-use short-lived magic link. The signing-workflow art ([US9286596B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9286596B2/en), [US20190050587A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20190050587A1/en), [US20240104299A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20240104299A1/en)) covers signing itself but none of those pages states heirs, division, or a downstream process gate. Third, **F6** — permitting finalization to complete while appraisals remain outstanding and stamping the printed Record of Decisions with the pending state and the escalating heir's name. Fourth, **F3 + F7 together** — a cross-application provenance record attributed to an import rather than a participant, plus a two-row revert that never deletes. The nearest fiduciary-control art, [US20050203815A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20050203815A1/en), is abandoned and does not recite this.

**Which mechanics have close prior art.** **F2 is the most exposed.** [US8812389B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8812389B2/en) claims exactly the target domain — dividing assets among heirs through sequential rounds including emotional-interest bidding and selection of the remainder — and although it is listed Expired - Fee Related, expiry removes enforceability, not prior-art effect. Layered on top are [US8577748B1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8577748B1) (draft allocation of items to group members under allocation rules), [US20080235113A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20080235113A1) (credit values independent of actual selling prices), [US6850918B1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6850918B1/en) (rounds of mutually undisclosed offers), and the auction-theory family [US7729975B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7729975B2/en) / [US20140304098A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20140304098A1/en). **R6 is similarly crowded**: [US10856807B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10856807B2/en) is active to 2038 and claims analyzing items from a sensed image through databases, APIs and machine learning; [US9495386B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9495386B2/en) and [US20100045423A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20100045423A1/en) add further coverage. **R10** is squarely covered in the generic by [US20210118435A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20210118435A1/en) and [US11042883B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11042883B2/en). **R5** is interesting in reverse: [US11861722B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11861722B2) claims automatic prediction of a user's possessions *with values attached* — Registry's rule is the deliberate negation of that, which is a distinguishing feature but also a reminder that the value-assignment space is occupied.

**Which mechanics are almost certainly not patentable standing alone.** **R7** (duplicate detection by title similarity plus a photo hash) is textbook record deduplication. **R8** (a prompt at the end of a room) is a user-interface nudge. **R9** (paper is authoritative) is a legal convention, not an invention — Fabric's "A printer, a pen, two witnesses" ([Fabric wills](https://meetfabric.com/wills)) and LegalZoom's "Then print and sign" ([LegalZoom bundle](https://www.legalzoom.com/personal/estate-planning/estate-planning-bundle.html)) show it is ordinary practice, and [US20150207786A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20150207786A1/en) already articulates the tangible-receipt-as-authority idea. **F8** (cookie-only, deny-by-default API auth with no identity from body/header/query) is security best practice and would read as conventional. **F10** (practice mode) is a sandbox flag. **F5** (absence of an aggregate threshold) is a negative limitation and cannot carry a claim by itself. **R1**'s two-list constraint is also a negative limitation — powerful as a dependent limitation, weak as an independent one.

**Recommended combinations for a defensible independent claim — for counsel to assess.** The strongest posture is a single claim that spans the app boundary, because no fetched patent or product does. A candidate shape: *a two-application system in which (a) an intake application records, per item, an owner-authored importance flag comprising a named recipient, a boolean, and a reason code selected from a closed enumeration, and a free-text comment bounded in length, where entry of the comment automatically sets the boolean; (b) the intake application renders a printable memorandum that reproduces the free text verbatim in a visually delimited region while omitting the reason code from the rendered output, and hard-suppresses any monetary estimate; (c) an export envelope conveys the flag and comment to a division application, which creates an audit record attributed to the import rather than to any participant identifier; (d) the division application refuses to open a preference-ranking phase until every non-administrative participant has signed a versioned, immutable snapshot of the method text; and (e) finalization emits a printed record that names, for each item under an outstanding third-party appraisal request, the participant who raised it.* That combination pulls its weight from R2/R3/R4 + F1 + F3 + F6 + F9, deliberately avoids resting on the ranked draft itself, and each of its distinguishing limitations is one that the fetched prior art either does not state or states in the opposite direction.

A useful secondary claim family would cover **the classification-provenance and revert mechanism (F3 + F7)** independently: an audit table in which a reclassification and its reversal persist as two rows with the original preserved, where the initiating identity may be a non-participant import source. And a third, narrower one around **F4 + F6**: an internal automated value estimate used solely to route a routing threshold, never rendered to non-administrative participants, coexisting with a participant-initiated request for an off-platform human appraisal that does not block finalization.

**Two caveats for counsel.** First, the searches here were conducted through Google Patents result pages and Justia assignee queries; the Google Patents structured query endpoint was unavailable during this session, so this is a screening scan, not a professional search — a formal search including EPO/WIPO families, CPC-class sweeps (G06Q 50/16, G06Q 40/00) and file-wrapper review is still required. Second, all of these mechanics sit in business-method territory where §101 eligibility, not just novelty, will be the gating question; the print-rendering and offline-capture limitations are the ones most likely to supply a technical hook.
